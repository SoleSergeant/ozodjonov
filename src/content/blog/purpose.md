Nothing we built exists without a reason.

A bridge is there so people can cross. A program is there to solve problem. A chair is there to hold someone up. Before we build something, we ask: what is this for?

If it doesn't serve anything, we throw it away.

I think that's the same with people. Each of our souls are given to serve something specific. We hold a specific purpose in life and everyone we meet have a specific purpose in our life.

We imagine purpose as something glorious: a title, applause, our name remembered. But as Loki learned, a glorious purpose usually turns out to be more burden than glory.

A bridge doesn't get applause. It carries weight every day, and people only notice it when it breaks.

That's what purpose really looks like: staying late to finish the work, keeping the team together, keeping a promise after everyone else has stopped caring. It costs you something. That's why it feels heavy.

The same is true for the people around us. Some come into our lives to carry us. Some come so we can carry them. Some stay for years, and some only for a moment, just long enough to teach us one thing.

No one is there by accident.

Sometimes I think about people who don't earn much, who the world doesn't call successful. The man sweeping the streets before sunrise. The farmer no one knows by name. The teacher in a small village school.

Then I think: someone has to do it. And they do it every day, without applause.

Maybe they are serving their purpose more faithfully than any of us. Their work is not smaller just because the world pays it less.

Success is how the world measures you. Purpose is how much you carry for others.

So the question isn't "What will make me great?"\
It's "What was I given to carry, and who was given to me?"

The glory may come late. It may never come. The weight comes first.

Find your purpose. Then serve it. And be grateful for the people who are serving theirs in your life.
