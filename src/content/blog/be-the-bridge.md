## You don't need a boss. You need a bridge.

Most people think a good team needs someone at the top giving orders. But the strongest teams often work differently: you don't have to be a leader, but you have to be a bridge.

## Different skills, different languages

People with different skills make a great team. But they often speak different languages. A programmer thinks in logic, a designer thinks in feeling, and a speaker thinks in audiences.

Left alone, each of them does good work in a separate corner. The bridge is the person who understands enough of every corner to translate between them. They know who is strong at what, who needs whom, and where the work should flow next.

When you connect everyone efficiently, something surprising happens. People stop waiting for instructions. They lead themselves.

## A bridge is still a leader

Being a bridge is not the easy way out of leadership. It is leadership done quietly. To translate between people, you have to understand each side well enough to earn their trust.

And sometimes the people on the bridge disagree about where it leads. Then someone has to make the call. A good bridge knows when to step back and when to step forward.

## Build bridges, not thrones

If you are on a team with people who are better than you at something, good. Your job isn't to outshine them. It's to connect them.

The best leaders spend most of their time as bridges and only sometimes as captains. Build the bridge well, and the team will cross it on its own.\
